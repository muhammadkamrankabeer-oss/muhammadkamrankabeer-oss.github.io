Chapter 1Computer NetworksOpen notes

Network types, topologies, devices, reference models, IP addressing and protocols

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

1.1 What is a network?

A computer network is a group of connected devices that share data and resources such as files, printers and an internet connection. Networks help people communicate, save cost and centralize data.

1.2 Types of networks

TypeFull formTypical area
PANPersonal Area NetworkAround one person, e.g. phone with earbuds
LANLocal Area NetworkRoom, home, school or office
MANMetropolitan Area NetworkA city or large campus
WANWide Area NetworkCountries or continents; the Internet is the largest example

1.3 Topologies and devices

A topology is the layout of a network.

  • Bus: all devices share one cable; cheap but a cable fault affects everyone.
  • Star: devices connect to a central switch or hub; easy to manage, but the centre is a single point of failure.
  • Ring: each device connects to two neighbours, forming a loop.
  • Mesh: devices have many links; reliable but costly.
  • Tree: star networks arranged in a hierarchy.
  • NIC: connects a device to a network.
  • Switch: forwards data to the intended device on a LAN.
  • Router: connects different networks and chooses paths for packets.
  • Modem: converts signals so a device can use an ISP line.

1.4 Reference models

The OSI model has seven layers: Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport, Session, Presentation and Application. The TCP/IP model groups them into four: Network Access, Internet, Transport and Application. Layers divide the work so each one has a clear job.

1.5 IP addresses and protocols

An IP address identifies a device on a network. IPv4 uses 32 bits (e.g. 192.168.1.10); IPv6 uses 128 bits. A protocol is an agreed set of rules for communication.

  • HTTP/HTTPS: web pages (HTTPS is encrypted).
  • FTP: file transfer.
  • SMTP: sending email.
  • DNS: converts domain names into IP addresses.
  • TCP: reliable delivery; UDP: fast delivery without guarantees.

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

A network connects devices so they can share data and resources. Networks are classified by size (PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN) and arranged in topologies such as bus, star, ring, mesh and tree. Switches and routers move data, layered models (OSI and TCP/IP) divide communication into tasks, IP addresses identify devices and protocols set the rules.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
NetworkConnected devices that share data and resources.
TopologyThe physical or logical layout of a network.
ProtocolA set of rules for communication between devices.
IP addressA number that identifies a device on a network.
DNSA service that maps domain names to IP addresses.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

Differentiate LAN and WAN.

A LAN covers a small area such as an office; a WAN covers a large area and connects LANs.

What is the function of a router?

It connects different networks and forwards packets towards their destination.

Why are layered models used?

They split communication into smaller tasks so each layer can be understood and developed separately.

Long question: Describe the star topology with advantages and disadvantages.

In a star topology every device connects to a central switch or hub. Advantages: easy to add devices, a faulty cable affects only one device, and management is simple. Disadvantages: if the central device fails the whole network stops, and more cable is needed than in a bus.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • LAN is small, WAN is large.
  • Router connects networks; switch connects devices inside a network.
  • IPv4 = 32 bits; IPv6 = 128 bits.

Quick Revision

Know the network types, topologies, OSI/TCP-IP layers and common protocols with their purpose.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not confuse a switch with a router.
  • Do not say HTTP and HTTPS are identical; HTTPS adds encryption.

Chapter 2Computational Thinking and AlgorithmsOpen notes

Decomposition, abstraction, algorithms, flowcharts, pseudocode and logic

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

2.1 Computational thinking

Computational thinking is a way of solving problems so that a computer or a person can carry out the solution. Its main parts are:

  • Decomposition: breaking a problem into smaller parts.
  • Pattern recognition: finding similarities or repeated ideas.
  • Abstraction: keeping important details and ignoring the rest.
  • Algorithm design: writing clear steps to solve the problem.

2.2 Algorithms

An algorithm is a finite, ordered set of clear steps that solves a problem. It can be shown as plain steps, pseudocode (structured, language-neutral text) or a flowchart (symbols and arrows).

START
read a, b
sum = a + b
print sum
END

2.3 Logic and truth tables

A proposition is a statement that is either true or false. Logical operators combine propositions.

ABA AND BA OR BNOT A
TTTTF
TFFTF
FTFTT
FFFFT

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

Computational thinking breaks a problem into parts, finds patterns, ignores unnecessary detail and produces an algorithm. Algorithms can be written as pseudocode or drawn as flowcharts. Logic uses true/false propositions and operators (AND, OR, NOT); truth tables list every possible result.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
DecompositionBreaking a problem into smaller, manageable parts.
AbstractionFocusing on essential details and hiding the rest.
AlgorithmA finite sequence of clear steps to solve a problem.
PseudocodeStructured, human-readable description of an algorithm.
Truth tableA table showing the output of a logical expression for all inputs.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

What is abstraction?

Keeping only the important details of a problem and ignoring unnecessary ones.

Differentiate pseudocode and flowchart.

Pseudocode uses text; a flowchart uses symbols and arrows.

What is a proposition?

A statement that is either true or false.

Long question: Explain the four parts of computational thinking with an example.

Take "make a school timetable". Decomposition splits it into subjects, teachers and rooms. Pattern recognition notices that every class follows the same weekly structure. Abstraction ignores unrelated details such as teachers’ hobbies. Algorithm design writes the steps for placing each lesson in a free slot.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • An algorithm must be clear and finite.
  • A proposition is true or false, never both.
  • A truth table with n inputs has 2ⁿ rows.

Quick Revision

Decompose, find patterns, abstract, then write the algorithm. Practise truth tables for AND, OR and NOT.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not mix up abstraction with hiding all information.
  • Do not skip rows in a truth table.

Chapter 3Object Oriented Programming Using PythonOpen notes

Classes, objects, attributes, methods and the four OOP principles

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

3.1 Classes and objects

A class is a blueprint; an object is an instance created from it. Attributes store data and methods define behaviour. The __init__ method runs when an object is created, and self refers to the current object.

class Student:
    def __init__(self, name, marks):
        self.name = name
        self.marks = marks

    def show(self):
        print(self.name, self.marks)

s1 = Student("Ayesha", 85)
s1.show()

Output: Ayesha 85

3.2 Four principles of OOP

  • Encapsulation: bundling data and methods and controlling access to data.
  • Abstraction: showing only essential features.
  • Inheritance: a child class reuses the attributes and methods of a parent class.
  • Polymorphism: the same method name behaves differently for different objects.
class Animal:
    def speak(self):
        print("Sound")

class Dog(Animal):
    def speak(self):
        print("Bark")

Dog().speak()

Output: Bark (the child method overrides the parent method).

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

OOP organizes a program around objects. A class defines attributes and methods; objects are created from it. Encapsulation protects data, abstraction hides complexity, inheritance reuses code and polymorphism lets one method name work in different ways.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
ClassA blueprint for creating objects.
ObjectAn instance of a class.
MethodA function defined inside a class.
InheritanceAcquiring features of a parent class in a child class.
PolymorphismOne interface, many forms of behaviour.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

What is the role of __init__?

It initializes a new object’s attributes when it is created.

What is inheritance?

A mechanism by which a child class reuses a parent class’s members.

Differentiate class and object.

A class is the design; an object is a real instance made from it.

Long question: Explain the four principles of OOP.

Encapsulation groups data and methods and limits direct access. Abstraction exposes only what is needed. Inheritance lets a new class build on an existing one. Polymorphism lets different classes provide their own version of the same method.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • Python methods take self as the first parameter.
  • An object is created by calling the class: Student("Ali", 70).
  • Child classes can override parent methods.

Quick Revision

Class = blueprint, object = instance. Remember encapsulation, abstraction, inheritance and polymorphism.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not forget self in method definitions.
  • Do not confuse a class with an object.

Chapter 4Development of Graphical User Interface (GUI)Open notes

GUI concepts, widgets, layout and event handling

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

4.1 What is a GUI?

A Graphical User Interface lets users interact with a program through windows, buttons, text boxes and menus instead of typing commands. Common parts (widgets) include labels, entry boxes, buttons and checkboxes.

4.2 Event-driven programming

A GUI waits for events such as a click or key press. An event handler (callback) is the function that runs in response. A main loop keeps the window open and listens for events.

import tkinter as tk

def greet():
    label.config(text="Hello, " + entry.get())

root = tk.Tk()
root.title("Greeting")
entry = tk.Entry(root)
entry.pack()
tk.Button(root, text="Greet", command=greet).pack()
label = tk.Label(root, text="")
label.pack()
root.mainloop()

This example uses Python’s standard tkinter library.

4.3 Good interface design

  • Keep the layout simple and consistent.
  • Use clear labels and readable text.
  • Give feedback after user actions.
  • Validate user input before using it.

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

A GUI lets users work with visual elements. Widgets are the building blocks, layout managers arrange them, and event handlers respond to user actions while the main loop keeps the program running. Good design is simple, consistent and checks input.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
GUIAn interface that uses graphical elements such as windows and buttons.
WidgetA GUI element such as a button, label or entry box.
EventAn action such as a click or key press.
Event handlerA function that runs when an event occurs.
Main loopA loop that keeps the window active and waits for events.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

What is a widget?

A visual element of a GUI, such as a button or label.

What is an event handler?

A function that runs in response to an event.

Why is a main loop needed?

It keeps the window open and continuously listens for events.

Long question: Explain event-driven programming in a GUI.

In event-driven programming the program does not run in a fixed order. It waits for events from the user, such as clicking a button. When an event occurs, the linked handler function runs, updates the interface if needed and control returns to the main loop.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • GUI programs are event-driven.
  • Widgets are placed using a layout method.
  • Always validate user input.

Quick Revision

Window → widgets → layout → event handlers → main loop.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not forget to start the main loop.
  • Do not call the handler with () inside command=, or it runs immediately.

Chapter 5Code Testing and DebuggingOpen notes

Types of errors, testing levels and debugging techniques

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

5.1 Types of errors

ErrorMeaningExample
Syntax errorCode breaks the language rules; program will not runMissing colon after if
Runtime errorError occurs while runningDividing by zero
Logic errorProgram runs but gives wrong outputUsing + instead of *

5.2 Testing

Testing checks whether software behaves as expected. A test case has an input and an expected output.

  • Unit testing: tests one function or module.
  • Integration testing: tests parts working together.
  • System testing: tests the complete program.
  • Acceptance testing: checks whether it meets the user’s needs.
  • Black-box: tests without seeing the code; white-box: tests with knowledge of the code.

5.3 Debugging

Debugging is finding and fixing the cause of an error. Useful techniques: read the error message and line number, add print() statements, use breakpoints and step through code, and test small parts separately.

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

Errors are syntax, runtime or logic errors. Testing finds defects using test cases at several levels; debugging finds and fixes their causes using error messages, print statements and debuggers. Retest after every fix.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
BugA defect in a program.
Test caseAn input with its expected output used to check a program.
DebuggingLocating and fixing the cause of a defect.
Unit testingTesting a single function or module.
BreakpointA point where a debugger pauses execution.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

Differentiate syntax and logic errors.

A syntax error breaks language rules and stops the program; a logic error lets it run but gives wrong results.

What is a test case?

An input together with the output expected from the program.

Differentiate testing and debugging.

Testing detects problems; debugging finds and fixes their causes.

Long question: Explain the levels of testing.

Unit testing checks single functions. Integration testing checks that modules work together. System testing checks the whole program. Acceptance testing confirms the software meets user requirements.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • Logic errors give no error message.
  • Test normal, boundary and invalid inputs.
  • Retest after fixing a bug.

Quick Revision

Know the three error types, four testing levels and basic debugging steps.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not think no error message means the program is correct.
  • Do not fix many things at once; change and test one thing at a time.

Chapter 6Data and AnalysisOpen notes

Data, information, cleaning, descriptive statistics and visualization

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

6.1 Data and information

Data is raw facts and figures; information is data that has been processed to be meaningful. Data may be structured (tables) or unstructured (text, images, video).

6.2 Steps of data analysis

  1. Define the question.
  2. Collect data.
  3. Clean data (remove errors, duplicates and missing values).
  4. Analyse using calculations or tools.
  5. Visualize and communicate the results.

6.3 Descriptive statistics

Mean = sum ÷ count; median = middle value of ordered data; mode = most frequent value; range = maximum − minimum.

Example: for 2, 4, 4, 6, 9 the mean is 5, the median is 4, the mode is 4 and the range is 7.

6.4 Visualization

Use bar charts to compare categories, line charts for change over time, pie charts for parts of a whole and scatter plots for relationships between two variables. Label axes and give the chart a title.

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

Data becomes information after processing. Analysis follows a sequence: question, collection, cleaning, analysis and presentation. Mean, median, mode and range summarize data, and charts make patterns easy to see.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
DataRaw facts and figures.
InformationProcessed data that has meaning.
Data cleaningRemoving errors, duplicates and missing values.
MedianThe middle value of ordered data.
VisualizationShowing data as charts or graphs.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

Differentiate data and information.

Data is raw; information is processed data that is useful.

Why is data cleaning important?

Wrong or missing data produces wrong results.

Find the mean of 10, 20 and 30.

(10 + 20 + 30) ÷ 3 = 20.

Long question: Explain the steps of data analysis.

First define the question, then collect relevant data. Clean the data to remove errors, analyse it using statistics or tools, and finally present findings with clear charts and conclusions.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • Clean data before analysing.
  • Sort data before finding the median.
  • Choose a chart that fits the question.

Quick Revision

Question → collect → clean → analyse → visualize. Remember mean, median, mode and range.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not find the median without ordering the data.
  • Do not leave chart axes unlabelled.

Chapter 7Hypothesis TestingOpen notes

Null and alternative hypotheses, significance level, p-value and errors

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

7.1 What is a hypothesis?

A hypothesis is a testable claim about a population. Hypothesis testing uses sample data to decide whether the evidence supports or contradicts the claim.

  • Null hypothesis (H₀): states there is no effect or no difference.
  • Alternative hypothesis (H₁): states there is an effect or a difference.

7.2 Steps of a hypothesis test

  1. State H₀ and H₁.
  2. Choose the significance level (α), commonly 0.05.
  3. Collect sample data and calculate the test statistic and p-value.
  4. Compare: if p-value ≤ α, reject H₀; otherwise do not reject H₀.
  5. State the conclusion in plain words.

7.3 Errors

ErrorMeaning
Type IRejecting H₀ when it is actually true
Type IIFailing to reject H₀ when it is actually false

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

Hypothesis testing checks a claim using sample data. We assume the null hypothesis, calculate how likely the observed data would be, and compare the p-value with the significance level. A small p-value is evidence against H₀. Type I and Type II errors are the two possible mistakes.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
Null hypothesisThe claim of no effect or no difference.
Alternative hypothesisThe claim that an effect or difference exists.
Significance level (α)The probability of a Type I error that we accept.
p-valueThe probability of results at least as extreme as observed, if H₀ is true.
Type I errorRejecting a true null hypothesis.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

What is a null hypothesis?

A statement of no effect or no difference that is tested.

When do we reject H₀?

When the p-value is less than or equal to the significance level.

Differentiate Type I and Type II errors.

Type I rejects a true H₀; Type II fails to reject a false H₀.

Long question: Explain the steps of hypothesis testing.

State H₀ and H₁, choose α, collect data and compute the test statistic and p-value, compare p with α to reject or not reject H₀, and finally write the conclusion in simple words.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • Small p-value = strong evidence against H₀.
  • “Do not reject” does not mean “proved true”.
  • Common α is 0.05.

Quick Revision

H₀ vs H₁ → choose α → compute p-value → decide → conclude.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not say H₀ is “accepted as proved”; say “fail to reject”.
  • Do not mix up Type I and Type II errors.

Chapter 9Cybersecurity and Safe Digital CollaborationOpen notes

Threats, the CIA triad, authentication, encryption and safe collaboration

Practise Class 12 Computer Science MCQs →Back to chapter contents ↑

Short Notes

9.1 Cybersecurity and the CIA triad

Cybersecurity is protecting systems, networks and data from attack or misuse. Its core goals are the CIA triad:

  • Confidentiality: only authorized people can see data.
  • Integrity: data stays accurate and unaltered.
  • Availability: systems and data are available when needed.

9.2 Common threats

ThreatMeaningProtection
MalwareHarmful software such as viruses and ransomwareTrusted software, updates, antivirus
PhishingFake messages that steal informationCheck sender and links
Weak passwordsEasy-to-guess credentialsLong, unique passwords and MFA
Data lossInformation deleted or lostRegular backups

9.3 Protection tools

Authentication confirms identity (password, OTP, biometrics); multi-factor authentication (MFA) uses two or more proofs. Encryption converts data into unreadable form for anyone without the key. A firewall filters network traffic.

9.4 Safe digital collaboration

  • Share files only with the people who need them and set the correct permissions.
  • Use trusted platforms and keep accounts protected with MFA.
  • Do not share passwords or one-time codes.
  • Respect copyright and other people’s privacy.
  • Keep backups and verify links before opening them.

Chapter Summary in Easy Words

Cybersecurity protects confidentiality, integrity and availability. Malware, phishing and weak passwords are common threats; strong passwords, MFA, encryption, firewalls, updates and backups reduce risk. Safe collaboration means controlling access, using trusted tools and respecting others’ rights.

Important Definitions and Key Terms

TermEasy definition
CybersecurityProtection of systems, networks and data from attacks.
PhishingA trick that uses fake messages to steal information.
EncryptionConverting data into unreadable form using a key.
MFAAuthentication that needs two or more proofs of identity.
FirewallA system that filters network traffic.

Important Short and Long Questions with Answers

What is the CIA triad?

Confidentiality, integrity and availability.

What is phishing?

A deceptive attempt to steal information using fake messages or sites.

Why use multi-factor authentication?

A stolen password alone is not enough to access the account.

Long question: Describe safe practices for online collaboration.

Use trusted platforms, protect accounts with strong passwords and MFA, share files with correct permissions only, never share passwords or codes, verify links, keep backups and respect copyright and privacy.

These are revision questions based on chapter concepts, not guaranteed examination questions.

Key Points to Remember

  • CIA = confidentiality, integrity, availability.
  • Use unique passwords and MFA.
  • Never share one-time codes.

Quick Revision

Know the CIA triad, common threats with protections, and safe collaboration habits.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not reuse one password across accounts.
  • Do not click links in unexpected messages.
